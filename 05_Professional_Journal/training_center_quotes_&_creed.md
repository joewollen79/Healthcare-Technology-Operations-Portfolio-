# Core Operating Principles

## 1. Pretty, Not Practical

> This is my personal quote that I have used many times while training in Martial Arts. In Martial Arts you can have more performance based Arts. They are meant for that, beauty, showcasing and entertainment, much like aspects of IT. Some new tech can be very showy, very FOMO based, or "keeping up with the neighbors." This isn't inherently a bad thing, this is how things get promoted and exposed. However, is it Practical?
>
> If you are learning Martial Arts for self defense, performance will not help you, it must be grounded in combat. However, the combat portion is not necessarily desirable for everyone to see or experience, but it could be necessary in the proper environment.
>
> This is the same principle I apply in the Training-Center. There are things that are pretty (upgraded UIs, Graphs, newer tech sometimes beyond what is needed), but I also try to operate from the Practical side. Will it enhance the Network, traffic flow, security, decrease attack surfaces, decrease vulnerability, help with optimization and traffic flow in the network?
>
> If it is working and serving all the purposes and operating efficiently, is it necessary to add "pretty?"
>
> It also means to not overengineer to the point of stagnating progress on future projects. This can also lead to paralysis and stall the growth of the Training-Center.

## 2. You Get What You Practice

> Repeated habits become real capability.
>
> However, a deeper meaning of this is if you don't practice you are still practicing. You are practicing procrastination, laziness, excuses, thus "You get what you practice."
>
> This also doesn't mean that one must only work, this is also an extreme. The overall goal of this is to practice moderation, rest and work, stability and instability, security and insecurity, function and dysfunction.
>
> The whole basis of all my previous training, Yin and Yang, many of these seem contradictory to IT, Cybersecurity and Network Engineering on the surface, but nature doesn't exist in a vacuum and one begets the other.
>
> In order for a Network to maintain stability we must account for the Human variable and how Humans may intentionally or unintentionally stress or destabilize the Network.
>
> That instability is what we must engineer stability for, the same with security.
>
> No matter how secure any system or network appears to be, we must constantly recognize there are always vulnerabilities to exploit and we must recognize this in order to enhance our own security. The same with function and dysfunction.
>
> So in Reality as Engineers and Security professionals we must ultimately think in terms of instability, insecurity, vulnerabilities, dysfunction. This is where we must live, then we can practice towards the counterbalance of these qualities.
>
> If you practice to only have security, stability and function, you will inevitably become blind to all the dysfunction and instability there really is in the system.
>
> We must be both microsystem and macrosystem oriented.

## 3. Enough

> How do you know when you fill a cup with enough water? When there is enough, that simple, not necessarily easy to practice, but simple.
>
> This is another foundational principle of this Training-Center.
>
> Is the Network stable enough to be pressure tested? Or am I spending days, weeks, months over engineering for all possible outcomes?
>
> This isn't feasible because then the Network may be stable, but it's hardly functional as it isn't even online.
>
> Same with Security. I can spend days and weeks trying to prevent every single vulnerability but this becomes so narrow in scope it is like watching one entrance for the intruder when you have several to be aware of.
>
> Hyperfocus is essential in completing tasks, but unnecessary for constant practical application.
>
> It is with these ideas this Training-Center is founded upon.
>
> I Engineer and make it Secure enough to be stable, safe, operational and functional and then work with the understanding that there will always be holes, things missed.
>
> But as I mentioned before, this is where we are truly tested as Engineers and Security professionals, this is where skills mature.
>
> By Engineering and making things Secure enough to move on and not get lost in over engineering, planning, or hypersensitivity we allow growth to occur as well.

## 4. Reading → Meaning → Principle → Ground

> "The way to study texts is not to strive after literary accomplishments and thereby confuse your eyes (mind). Instead, you must extract the Meaning as it harmonizes with the Heart. Abandon texts after you have extracted their meaning and grasped their Principle. Abandon the Principle after you have realized the Fundamental Ground. After you have Realized the Fundamental Ground then attend to it until it completely enters the Heart."  
> ~ Wang Chong Yang (From Quanzhen Taoist Scripture)
>
> Read, study, gaining knowledge, all these are foundational and important upon starting any endeavor or journey.
>
> This is where I currently am, trying to learn, understand and study.
>
> However, if we only stay here, there is no growth, within practical application do we develop skills and wisdom.
>
> This is where reading and study fail, we must get to a deeper meaning of what we are working with.
>
> What does it really mean when Frames flow from Hop to Hop? What does it mean when a Router de-encapsulates a Frame? How is this useful (or exploitable for Security)? Can this be hijacked and altered?
>
> As you begin to understand the meaning of Traffic flow you develop a deeper meaning and understanding.
>
> However, this must be taken deeper to understanding the main principle is that Frames must exist for a period of time as a Frame from Hop to Hop, but then they are both simultaneously the old frame (from the previous Hop) and a new frame (on the new Hop) being built to a future frame (the next Hop).
>
> Any of these can be points of failure, instability, insecurity.
>
> The principle that the frame is always being de-encapsulated and encapsulated simultaneously speaks constantly of the deeper Fundamental Ground of Change.
>
> Out of this Chaos in Change, we can begin to see where Engineering or Security can be necessary to establish the counter opposite of Order.

# Growth Through Difficulty

## 5. Invest in Loss

> Lose, fail, fall, break it all down, then learn from it.
>
> Why did it fail? What broke? Where did it all collapse?
>
> Then learn and work towards building deeper understanding. This assists in providing skills to either deal with the situation again in the future or possibly prevent it.
>
> Accept and allow failure to occur, for it is here where an individual will show their strength, their perseverance or endurance.
>
> Too often are we afraid of failure, loss, or destruction, this is just as important as construction, success and gain.
>
> This is where we as individuals can find the empowered meaning and turn this to an opportunity of growth.
>
> While it is never desirable to lose, possibly millions of dollars for a corporation, would it be desirable to invest millions into a future topology that doesn't topple with a minor change to the firewall? Or so a 12 year old with AI can break into your entire system?
>
> What if these vulnerabilities showed up in the future when it could cost billions? Or during a more critical and crucial time of the Company?
>
> These losses, these failures will happen, the point isn't to worry about them; you bring them on more.
>
> You fight them and you find more of them, you exhaust yourself, your team.
>
> You spend millions trying to avoid imaginary issues and "monsters."
>
> This is more of a waste than growing through adversity and investing in your team, your company, your employees.
>
> People or Companies that throw away money, time, effort chasing illusions won't last long.
>
> It is accepting and allowing Loss that we are truly able to invest and grow.
>
> *(Cheng Man Ch’ing principle)*
