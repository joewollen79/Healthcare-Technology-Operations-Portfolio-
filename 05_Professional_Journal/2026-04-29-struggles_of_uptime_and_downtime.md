I have been feeling the pressure of trying to manage uptime and downtime with the network. Even though I am operating more in a family environment, telehealth aside, it is surprising how much uptime is really needed in the modern family. Telehealth must be up daily for several hours, not just for VoIP but also for notes and documentation. In addition, my partner is also doing online classes and studying, as am I (obviously). This doesn't allow for downtime during the day when the kids are in school. Then when they get home, it is usually homework for a few hours, then they need their "downtime" (music, TV, etc.), and then before I know it, it's 10 PM. At night, we still have IoT devices that should be running (though not as vital as enterprise), but our alarm system, HVAC, kids have their sleep machines, etc.

This has really forced me to find optimal downtime and work within significant constraints to minimize downtime. This morning I was up at my usual 4 AM, but rather than training, I found it a good time to use for downtime. So I used that to prep WAN validation of each VLAN. During this time, though, I only had 20–30 minutes, so I used my prep time to set up and verify switch configurations and also perform OPNsense validation so that when I got downtime later today, I was able to do this as quickly as possible.

It is also good because I am finding some commands are starting to stick. It has been disheartening for a while when my network had a DHCP failure when I was running KEA initially because I didn't know any better and it wasn't the best choice for my beginning build. I have now switched to ISC to rebuild the entire network—I mean the entire network. I have completely isolated my network from the WAN, and this has been a valuable learning experience because it has helped take off so much pressure of having an outage. However, it has also shown that I can have more constraints, with downtime and uptime for WAN validation being one of the most significant ones. It is helping me learn to troubleshoot and also plan better while taking multiple variables into consideration.
